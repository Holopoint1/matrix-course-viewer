Duration: 60 minutes
Target group: Learners aged 16–21, apprentices, or new employees
Topic: Introduction to workplace health and safety

Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

  1. Explain why workplace health and safety is important.
  2. Identify common workplace hazards.
  3. Describe suitable control measures for reducing risk.
  4. Explain the responsibilities of employers and employees.
  5. Complete a simple workplace risk assessment.

Key teaching content

Hazard and risk

A hazard is something with the potential to cause harm.

Examples include:

  • Moving machinery
  • Electricity
  • Chemicals
  • Working at height
  • Manual handling
  • Slips, trips and falls
  • Noise
  • Fire
  • Poor workstation setup

A risk is the likelihood that the hazard will cause harm, combined with the possible severity of that harm.

Employer responsibilities

Employers should:

  • Provide a safe working environment.
  • Assess workplace risks.
  • Provide suitable training and supervision.
  • Maintain equipment in a safe condition.
  • Provide appropriate personal protective equipment.
  • Record and investigate accidents where required.
  • Provide emergency procedures and first-aid arrangements.

Employee responsibilities

Employees should:

  • Follow safety instructions and training.
  • Use equipment correctly.
  • Wear required PPE.
  • Report hazards, defects and accidents.
  • Avoid putting themselves or others at risk.
  • Cooperate with workplace safety procedures.

Hierarchy of control

Control measures should be considered in the following order:

  1. Eliminate: Remove the hazard completely.
  2. Substitute: Replace the hazard with something safer.
  3. Engineering controls: Use guards, barriers, extraction systems or automatic controls.
  4. Administrative controls: Use procedures, signs, training and restricted access.
  5. Personal protective equipment: Use gloves, safety footwear, eye protection or hearing protection.

PPE should normally be treated as the final level of protection rather than the first solution.

Differentiation

Support

  • Provide a list of common hazards.
  • Use photographs and symbols.
  • Pair less-confident learners with supportive peers.
  • Provide sentence starters such as: “The hazard is…” and “The risk could be reduced by…”

Challenge

Ask more-confident learners to:

  • Rate each risk as low, medium or high.
  • Suggest controls using the hierarchy of control.
  • Explain why PPE alone may not adequately control the hazard.
  • Consider how vulnerable people could be affected.

Plenary questions

Learners answer the following questions independently:

  1. What is the difference between a hazard and a risk?
  2. Give one responsibility of an employer and one responsibility of an employee.
  3. Identify one workplace hazard and state an appropriate control measure.

Success criteria

Learners have achieved the lesson objectives when they can:

  • Correctly identify at least three workplace hazards.
  • Explain the difference between hazard and risk.
  • Suggest a suitable control measure.
  • State at least one employer and one employee responsibility.
  • Contribute to a basic risk assessment.

Optional follow-up task

Ask learners to carry out a visual safety inspection of a classroom, workshop or simulated workplace. They should identify three hazards and recommend suitable control measures.
